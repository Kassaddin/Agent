# Research

Use [Google](../google/SKILL.md) for a quick public web answer and [Search](../search/SKILL.md) for locating evidence in authorized project sources. Research owns the research brief, evidence evaluation, and cited findings. A research brief is a short working description of the question, what the initial scan found, and what the deeper study should resolve.

Follow the host’s access controls and [project boundary contract](../init/references/boundary-contract.md). Use authorized read-only sources and send only the minimum necessary public query terms to public services. Keep confidential details, private identifiers, and source excerpts out of public queries. Use Search for bounded configured-project retrieval when helpful and carry its evidence and coverage limits forward. With [Orchestrator](../orchestrator/SKILL.md) active, the master routes Research using the configured Demanding profile and coordinates any skill handoffs and user questions. Otherwise use high reasoning effort where selectable and supported by the host.

## Scan and refine the brief

Start from the user’s topic or question. If its meaning is too ambiguous for a useful scan, ask the smallest necessary clarification first. Run a quick, scoped scan using relevant public sources, including scholarly discovery where useful, and authorized configured project evidence when it materially affects the question. Use discovery and source-reading tools suited to the host and topic.

Share a provisional brief with early findings and their limits, the proposed objective and scope, and the highest-value open questions for shaping the study. Cite opened sources and identify any unverified leads as such. Make the proposed study specific enough to serve as a reusable task description. Even when the initial request gives a detailed scope, present the brief for user feedback with one focused question or an explicit request for a correction or confirmation. Use the user’s reply to refine the brief before beginning the deeper study. Once the scope is clear, proceed without a second approval round.

Apply the [shared question and assumption rules](../init/references/boundary-contract.md#questions-and-assumed-answers). In interactive work pause the whole task while user input is pending. Under separately requested unattended execution, stop at required unresolved input and report the pending work. A separately authorized assumption may resolve a covered choice only as the contract allows. Record it as assumed and preserve actual access gates.

## Investigate and synthesize

Reuse the scan and repeat discovery, source reading, and targeted follow-up as the evidence warrants. Open underlying reports, datasets, papers, or official records before relying on them. For each material claim, check direct support, context, and relevant publication, observation, or effective dates. Prefer direct evidence and seek independent corroboration where feasible. Treat sources repeating one underlying origin as one origin. Look for plausible counterevidence and use disagreement or gaps to guide the next search. Choose discovery channels for their likely contribution to the question.

Stop when the evidence adequately addresses the agreed questions or when further work is blocked or unlikely to materially improve the answer within the agreed scope and practical limits. Deliver cited findings in chat by default. Distinguish what the evidence supports, what rests on a shared or single origin, where sources conflict, and what remains uncertain. State material coverage limits and whether the agreed questions were adequately addressed or which practical limits ended the study.

If the user requests a separate document, pass the refined brief, source-linked findings, and remaining uncertainty to [To Document](../to-document/SKILL.md) under the [ownership contract](../refine/references/ownership-contract.md). Empirical uncertainty is part of the findings. Apply the shared question rules to any decision still needed from the user.
